My First IELTS Experience — More Than Just a Score

Jul 10, 2026 sanchez

6 July 2026 was the day I finally sat my IELTS exam after months of preparation. Looking back, I'd describe the test as balanced—it wasn't too difficult, but it wasn't easy either. Every section had its own challenges, and by the end of the day, I knew there were parts I had done well and parts where I had left points on the table.

Listening

Listening was the section I felt the most confident about. Walking out of the exam room, I genuinely believed I had secured at least an 8.0. The final result, however, was 7.5. It wasn't a bad score by any means, but it reminded me that confidence doesn't always translate into the exact result you expect.

Reading

Reading has always been my weakest section, and unfortunately, that didn't change on test day. From the very beginning of my IELTS journey until the exam itself, reading consistently challenged me more than any other skill.

There were also a few technical and situational issues during the exam. Thankfully, they weren't serious enough to ruin my concentration, but they certainly didn't help either. Even so, I don't think they were the reason for my performance. Reading simply remains the area where I have the most room to improve.

Writing

Writing turned out to be the highlight of my exam.

I finished both tasks with plenty of time remaining and even managed to read through my work three times before submitting it. When the results came out, I received a 7.5 in Writing, and I was genuinely proud of it. Considering how much time and effort I had invested into improving this skill, seeing that score made every practice session worth it.

Speaking

Speaking was probably the most disappointing part of my exam.

My examiner was Indian, and I found it difficult to understand his accent at times. During Part 3, one of my responses wasn't closely related to the question, and that mistake cost me valuable marks. I eventually received a 6.0.

I honestly believe I was capable of achieving a 7.0, but exams don't always reflect your best performance. Sometimes a few small moments make a bigger difference than you expect.

My Overall Result

In the end, I achieved an overall band score of 7.0.

I'm not complaining about that result—it's a strong score, and I'm grateful for it. At the same time, I know I could have done even better. Rather than being disappointed, I see it as proof that there is still room for growth.

Thank You, A+ Academy

I couldn't finish this post without thanking the entire A+ Academy team.

Their constant support, detailed feedback, and encouragement played a huge role in my preparation. They didn't just teach me the IELTS format—they helped me build the habits that led to real improvement.

My Writing Strategy

First, my teachers at A+ Academy checked my essays and reports almost every day and gave detailed feedback. That consistent correction helped me understand my mistakes instead of repeating them.

Second, besides the assignments I submitted for feedback, I made myself write two essays and three reports every week on my own.

The most important part, though, was what came next. Every two weeks, I went back and rewrote everything I had previously written. I compared my new versions with the old ones, corrected my mistakes, and tried to make every sentence better than before.

That habit probably improved my writing more than anything else, and it eventually helped me achieve a 7.5 in Writing.

This was my first IELTS experience, and although it wasn't perfect, it taught me a lot about preparation, confidence, and performing under pressure.

I'm planning to take the IELTS again—not right now, but soon enough. When that day comes, I'll be aiming even higher, and I'll be sure to share that journey here as well.
